\section*{Introduction}

\begin{parag}{Overview of the course}
	Quantum information processing is a sort of unification of the two followings fields: \textbf{physics} and \textbf{computer science}/ \textbf{communication}. Quantum physics is a new field of physics that emerged around 1900/1930, we found that that the behaviour of particle at a really small scale was really different of what we expected, uncertainty was the new discovery at atomic scale. We are not able to exactly predict how a particle behave even by knowing every information that could possibly be known.
\end{parag}

\section{References}

\begin{parag}{Bibliographie}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Michel Le Bellac}: \textit{A short introduction to quantum information and quantum computation}, Cambridge University press 2006. A small pedagogical book introducing physical aspects of the subject.
        \item \textit{N. David Mermin}: \textit{Quantum Computer Science, An introduction}, Cambridge University press 2007. An introduction written by a physicist for computer scientists.
        \item \textit{Neil Gershenfeld, The Physics of Information Technology}, Cambridge University Press 2000, An introduction to various phenomena, classical and quantum, underpinning information technologies.
        \item \textit{Michael A. Nielsen and Isaac Chuang, Quantum Computation and Quantum Information}, Cambridge University Press 2000. Complete reference probably somewhat more advanced than these lectures.
    \end{itemize}
    
\end{parag}
